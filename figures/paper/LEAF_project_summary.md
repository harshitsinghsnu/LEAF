# LEAF: Project Summary

## Research Objective

Determine whether LLM-driven evolutionary molecular proposal earns its cost over conventional, non-agentic search — under identical evaluation oracle, acquisition function, and query budget — using real ionizable-lipid design (Ugi three-component-reaction chemistry, AGILE dataset) as the testbed. The goal was never to design better lipids; it was to isolate one variable (the proposal mechanism) and measure whether swapping in an LLM changes search efficiency, holding everything else fixed.

## Research Questions

- **RQ1 (primary):** Does an LLM Proposer achieve higher Pareto hypervolume than a static genetic algorithm or random search, under a matched oracle-query budget and identical acquisition function?
- **RQ2:** Does Hypothesis Ledger feedback (literature/attribution-grounded reasoning fed back into proposals) improve sample efficiency?
- **RQ3:** Does debate-based, conformally-calibrated toxicity verification reduce false acceptance relative to a simple binary filter?
- **RQ4:** Can the system rediscover a known structure-property association (brominated tails/potency) from literature grounding, conditional on no pretraining leakage?
- **RQ5:** Is the frozen evaluation oracle itself valid (does it generalize to held-out molecules) across increasingly hard extrapolation regimes?
- **RQ6:** Is any observed effect an artifact of an easy search space (budget ≫ space size), or a genuine result under real scarcity?

## What We Did (Method)

1. **Built a real, budget-accounted active-learning loop** (Algorithm 1): a frozen Gaussian-process oracle (Tanimoto kernel, fit once on real AGILE HeLa/RAW264.7 potency labels, frozen before any search) stands in for a laboratory; a separate GP surrogate refits every round on acquired labels only; a shared UCB (later also EHVI) acquisition function selects which proposed candidates actually charge the query budget, identically across every proposer.
2. **Isolated the proposal mechanism as the only free variable**: four proposers — an LLM (four different models: Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen2.5 7B, Qwen2.5 14B, Gemini Flash-Lite), a static genetic algorithm, uniform random search, and a Thompson-sampling bandit — were run under byte-identical acquisition, budget, and vocabulary access.
3. **Extended the real search space** from AGILE's 1,200 labeled lipids to 30,044 real, chemically valid combinations (by inverting AGILE's own unlabeled virtual-screening library), so the budget (B=40) represents real scarcity (~0.13% of the space), not an easy, nearly-exhaustible problem.
4. **Validated the oracle** on three splits of increasing difficulty (random, scaffold-cold, chemotype-cold) against a pre-registered validity gate (held-out Spearman ρ≥0.3) before trusting any downstream comparison.
5. **Instrumented the loop directly** rather than inferring mechanism: logged per-round and per-query duplicate rates, edit distances to the nearest archive member, applicability-domain (fragment-novelty) counts, and reject rates — so claims about *why* a model underperforms are measured, not guessed.
6. **Ran real ablations** on the two secondary agentic components (a debate/conformal toxicity Verifier, a literature-grounded Hypothesis Ledger) to test whether they contribute anything beyond the core proposer, rather than assuming they do.
7. **Audited our own pipeline**: caught and fixed three real implementation bugs (a full-data oracle leaking split information, a genetic algorithm's mutation rate unmatched to the LLM's, and an acquisition-function dispatch bug that silently ran UCB whenever EHVI was configured) — reporting the correction rather than the original, more favorable-looking result.
8. **Responded to external review with new real experiments**, not just discussion: a HeLa-only single-objective reanalysis (removing a failing-validity secondary readout), a stricter de-duplication control (excluding all previously-evaluated candidates, not just the current archive), an objective-scored prompt variant, a rank-only prompt variant, a bare-LLM leakage probe, and a CoT-rationale requirement — each designed to rule out one specific alternative explanation for an observed effect.

## What We Got (Results)

- **RQ1 — no.** Across four real LLMs, eight seeds, three splits: no configuration shows a significant, family-wise-corrected advantage over static-GA or random search. Two small models (Llama 8B, Qwen 7B) show a significant *deficit* on most comparisons. A larger model (Qwen2.5 14B) removes the specific failure mechanism (duplication) but establishes neither superiority nor equivalence. Holm correction leaves at most one comparison significant out of the primary eight, and six post-hoc equivalence tests all fail — the honest summary is "no demonstrated improvement," not "proven inferiority" or "proven parity."
- **Mechanism, not just outcome:** 79.1% of one model's real, budget-charging queries exactly duplicated an already-evaluated candidate — worse than the raw proposal-pool rate (67.8%), meaning acquisition concentrates on duplicates rather than filtering them. This is a measured, not inferred, explanation for the deficit.
- **The mechanism is fixable, partially:** removing duplicates (matched across all methods) narrows the deficit and can flip medians positive at reduced sample sizes, but increases proposal cost 2.3–7× and can leave the budget unspent entirely for some seeds — a real trade-off, not a free win.
- **Information, not reasoning format, drives the biggest gains found:** giving the LLM real objective scores, or even just relative rank (no magnitudes), moved medians further than requiring explicit chain-of-thought reasoning with no new information. Ruled out via three targeted experiments: a leakage probe (no zero-shot predictive skill exists to "reactivate"), a rank-only variant (matches the scored-prompt effect without exposing magnitudes), and a CoT-only variant (helps much less than either).
- **RQ2/RQ3 — untested, not negative, and the two components differ in how.** The Verifier was enabled (`use_verifier: true`) in every single experiment in the project, with no exception, but is deterministic throughout: AGILE has no real toxicity assay, so `cal_scores = cal_labels` exactly, producing zero-width intervals and no genuine epistemic signal — always active, never contributory. The Hypothesis Ledger is a real split: it was enabled in the primary sweep and early ablations, where it stayed empty across all 72 runs (a structural property of Pareto-restricted counterfactual matching with a fixed control value, verified directly), then was *deliberately disabled* (`use_hypothesis: false`), not silently left off, in every later isolation experiment (de-duplication, EHVI, the single-assay reanalysis, the enriched-prompt checks) to focus evaluation on the proposer. Neither finding shows these components would fail to help under different conditions (a real toxicity assay; non-Pareto-restricted matching) — only that they didn't operate under the tested ones.
- **RQ4 — not run.** No halogenated reagent exists in the real vocabulary, so the one target association this component was designed to test couldn't be constructed.
- **RQ5 — yes, mostly.** The oracle passes its validity gate on 5 of 6 (split × readout) combinations; the one failure (RAW264.7 on chemotype-cold) was explicitly excluded from that split's primary interpretation via a real single-objective reanalysis, which preserved the same qualitative deficit at a smaller, now fully-interpretable scale.
- **RQ6 — no.** The negative result survives across three sequential budget/space conditions (B=400 on 1,200 candidates; B=80 on 1,200; B=80/40 on 30,044) — not an artifact of an easy search space.

## What This Means

LEAF's evaluated contribution is a matched-budget audit, not a working improved lipid-design system: no tested LLM configuration, model, prompt variant, or acquisition function establishes a proposal advantage, but the paper also does not claim LLMs categorically cannot help — only that under this specific zero-shot, no-fine-tuning, information-scarce setup, they don't. The architecture is agentic by design (multiple coordinating LLM-driven components), but the empirically active and tested contribution is the Proposer alone. Precisely: the Verifier ran in every experiment but was non-contributory (no real toxicity assay yields zero-width conformal intervals); the Hypothesis Ledger was enabled in the primary sweep, where it remained empty for structural reasons, and was deliberately disabled in later isolation experiments. The paper should say this in one explicit sentence rather than leaving it to be inferred from scattered disclosures.
